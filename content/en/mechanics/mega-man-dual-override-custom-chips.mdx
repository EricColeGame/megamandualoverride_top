## The Confirmed Systems Any Build Rests On

Before you speculate about slots and stat rolls, understand the three mechanics Capcom has actually detailed. These are the systems that would interact with **Mega Man Dual Override custom chips** if such a layer exists.

**The Override System** lets a robot release its limiter for a short burst of strength beyond normal limits. The tradeoff is severe: once Override ends, the character is left vulnerable, with several functions performing at reduced levels. Timing is everything — pop it too early against a boss and you'll spend the dangerous part of the fight weakened.

**Special Weapons** are Mega Man's reward for defeating Robot Masters. Each has unique characteristics and drains weapon energy, unlike the unlimited Mega Buster. **Shield Actions** are Proto Man's equivalent: he analyzes combat data from defeated Robot Masters and adds effects to his Proto Shield, executed through button combinations, with an overheat penalty for overuse.

| System | Hero | Resource Cost | Risk Profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mega Buster | Mega Man | None | Low — reliable chip damage |
| Special Weapons | Mega Man | Weapon energy | Medium — resource management |
| Proto Shield Actions | Proto Man | Heat buildup | High — punishable if spammed |
| Override | Both | Cooldown + weakened state | Very high — timing dependent |

A customization layer built around **Mega Man Dual Override custom chips** would almost certainly target these three pillars: Override duration, weapon energy economy, and heat management. That's the most logical design space.

## Mega Man vs Proto Man: Two Different Build Philosophies

The two heroes don't just look different — they ask different questions of the player. Mega Man is the straightforward, ranged-pressure character: gentle by nature, strong sense of justice, armed with the Mega Buster and an expanding arsenal of Special Weapons. His ceiling comes from knowing which weapon answers which boss.

Proto Man is the technical pick. His shield drives a variety of attacks, and his kit rewards execution and button-combo fluency rather than raw output. He's also the one with the yellow scarf and the overheat mechanic, which means a single misjudged sequence can lock you out of your best options.

If a chip system does arrive, these differences matter. Community reports from similar games suggest players gravitate toward shoring up weaknesses first — which for Proto Man means heat management, and for Mega Man means weapon energy sustain.
